# SECTION A - CIE-2 (DEC 2025) PART A: 10 MCQs SOLVED

**1. Which Dosa is primarily responsible for movement in the body?**
(a) Pitta (b) Kapha (c) Vata (d) Tejas

**Answer: (c) Vata**

Vata is the principle of movement (Air + Ether). It governs nerve impulses, circulation, respiration, peristalsis and elimination. Pitta is transformation (digestion, metabolism); Kapha is cohesion (structure, lubrication, immunity).

---

**2. The treatise that governs Vastu Sastra principles is:**
(a) Natya Sastra (b) Manasara (c) Sushruta Samhita (d) Rigveda

**Answer: (b) Manasara**

Manasara and Mayamata are the two classical treatises on Vastu Shastra. Natya Sastra (Bharata) is on drama and aesthetics; Sushruta Samhita is on surgery; the Rigveda is a Vedic Samhita.

---

**3. The basic unit of Indian town planning in Vastu is:**
(a) Mangala (b) Karmic grid (c) Pada (d) Prana grid

**Answer: (c) Pada**

The site is divided into a grid of **padas** (squares) in the Vastu Purusha Mandala (commonly 64 or 81 squares). **Pada Vinyasa** is the laying out of the site on this grid, so the pada is the basic module of planning.

---

**4. The Eight Limbs of Vastu are known as:**
(a) Ashtanga Vastu (b) Saddarsana (c) Pancha Mahabhuta (d) Mantra Sastra

**Answer: (a) Ashtanga Vastu**

Ashta = eight, anga = limb. The eight limbs are Sthapatya, Vastu, Prasada, Yana, Shayana, Asana, Ayudha and Abharana. Saddarsana = the six schools of philosophy; Pancha Mahabhuta = the five elements.

---

**5. The temple style with curvilinear Shikhara is:**
(a) Nagara (b) Dravidian (c) Vesara (d) Ajanta

**Answer: (a) Nagara**

The Nagara (North Indian) style has a curvilinear **shikhara** over the sanctum, with amalaka and kalasha on top. Dravida has a pyramidal **vimana** with gopurams; Vesara is the hybrid Deccan style.

---

**6. The ratio of circumference to diameter is known as:**
(a) e (b) pi (c) phi (d) theta

**Answer: (b) pi**

pi = circumference / diameter, approximately 3.14159. Indian values: Shulba Sutras about 3.0888, Aryabhata 3.1416, Madhava 3.14159265359. (e is the base of natural logarithms, phi the golden ratio.)

---

**7. Aryabhata proposed that Earth:**
(a) Is stationary (b) Rotates on its axis (c) Orbits the Sun annually (d) Is flat

**Answer: (b) Rotates on its axis**

In the Aryabhatiya (499 CE) he stated that the Earth is spherical and rotates on its own axis, producing day and night, and that the stars only *appear* to move. Note that he did not propose that the Earth orbits the Sun annually - that is a different claim, so option (c) is wrong.

---

**8. Which metal was used first in ancient India?**
(a) Iron (b) Copper (c) Steel (d) Aluminium

**Answer: (b) Copper**

Copper was the earliest metal worked in the subcontinent (Indus Valley artefacts), used for tools, vessels, ornaments and idols. Iron appears from about 1200 BCE; steel and wootz come later. Aluminium is a modern metal.

---

**9. Which Yantra is used for measuring time?**
(a) Gola-yantra (b) Chakra-yantra (c) Water clock (Ghati Yantra) (d) Shanku-yantra

**Answer: (c) Water clock (Ghati Yantra)**

The Ghati Yantra is a bowl-shaped water clock with a hole at the bottom; the flow of water measures time intervals (1 ghati = 24 minutes). Gola is an armillary sphere, Chakra measures angular height, Shanku is a gnomon for shadows.

---

**10. The Chandasutras are fundamentally related to:**
(a) Geometry (b) Meter of poetry (c) Planetary motion (d) Aromatic chemicals

**Answer: (b) Meter of poetry**

Chandas = prosody, the metre of poetry. Pingala's Chandahsastra analyses metres built from Laghu and Guru syllables, and in doing so introduces binary representation, prastara, nashta, uddista, meru prastara and matrameru.

## Question 1

**1a. Derive the nth term of an arithmetic progression. (3 marks)**

An arithmetic progression (AP) is a sequence in which each term is obtained by adding a fixed number **d** (the common difference) to the previous term. Let the first term be **a**.

```
a1 = a
a2 = a + d          (d added once)
a3 = a + 2d         (d added twice)
a4 = a + 3d         (d added thrice)
...
an = a + (n - 1)d   (d added (n - 1) times)
```

Therefore the general formula for the nth term is:

```
an = a + (n - 1) d
```

The nth term is obtained by adding (n - 1) times the common difference to the first term.

Example: for 3, 7, 11, 15, ... a = 3 and d = 4, so a10 = 3 + 9 x 4 = 39.

Also useful: the sum of the first n terms is Sn = n/2 [2a + (n - 1)d].

**1b. Mention any two Panchanga elements. (2 marks)**

Panchanga means "five limbs". Two elements (any two of the five):

- **Tithi** - the lunar day, defined by a 12-degree increase in the angular separation of the Moon and the Sun; 30 tithis per lunar month.
- **Vara** - the weekday, named after the planets (Ravi-vara, Soma-vara, Mangala-vara, Budha-vara, Guru-vara, Shukra-vara, Shani-vara).
- **Nakshatra** - the lunar constellation; the ecliptic is divided into 27 nakshatras of 13 degrees 20 minutes each.
- **Yoga** - an auspicious or inauspicious period, computed from the sum of solar and lunar longitudes (27 yogas).
- **Karana** - half of a tithi (6 degrees), used for precise ritual timing (11 karanas).

**1c. What is Wootz steel? (2 marks)**

Wootz steel is a **high-carbon crucible steel** produced in ancient India (c. 300 BCE - 1700 CE). It is renowned for its exceptional **hardness, toughness and sharp edge retention**. Wootz was made by melting wrought iron with carbon-rich materials in **sealed clay crucibles**, followed by slow cooling. This process led to a characteristic **watered or damask pattern** due to carbide (cementite) formations. Wootz steel was widely exported and later became famous in the Middle East as **Damascus steel** for swords and blades.

---

**1d. Explain Pingala's contribution to combinatorics. (3 marks)**

Acharya Pingala, in his **Chandahsastra** (c. 200 BCE), made foundational contributions to combinatorics and binary mathematics through the study of poetic metres. Key contributions:

- **Binary representation of syllables**: Laghu (short) and Guru (long), analogous to 0 and 1.
- **Systematic enumeration** of all possible metrical patterns for a given number of syllables (**prastara**); a line of n syllables has 2^n patterns.
- **Development of methods equivalent to permutations and combinations** - the number of patterns with k gurus among n syllables is the binomial coefficient C(n,k).
- **Recursive counting techniques similar to the Fibonacci sequence**, known as **matrameru**.
- **Early use of ideas equivalent to Pascal's triangle**, known as **meru prastara**.
- His arrangement of the eight Ganas (three-syllable words) forms a binary cycle, in modern terms a **de Bruijn sequence**.

## Question 2

**2a. Describe the role of geometry in temple construction. (3 marks)**

Geometry plays a central role in Indian temple architecture, ensuring **structural stability, aesthetic harmony and spiritual symbolism**.

- Temples are designed using the **Vastu Purusha Mandala**, a geometric grid (usually 64 or 81 squares) that governs proportions and spatial layout.
- Precise geometric ratios determine the **orientation**, the **base plan (adhisthana)**, the **elevation (shikhara or vimana)** and the **symmetry** of the temple.
- Geometry also **symbolises cosmic order**, linking the microcosm (the temple) with the macrocosm (the universe). The garbhagriha is the centre of the mandala, and the whole structure is a scaled model of the cosmos.
- Practical geometry (from the Shulba Sutra tradition) was used for setting out right angles, squares, circles and equal-area transformations, and for units such as tala, mana, hasta and dhanus.

---

**2b. Describe the art of making natural perfumes. (3 marks)**

The art of making natural perfumes in India is known as **Gandha Shastra**, and it involves extracting fragrances from flowers, woods, resins, roots and spices. Traditional methods include:

- **Hydro-distillation** using water and heat (for example, rose water).
- **Steam distillation** - the **deg-bhapka** method: material is heated in a sealed copper pot (deg) and the vapour is condensed in a receiver (bhapka).
- **Enfleurage** - using fats to absorb fragrance from delicate flowers.
- **Infusion / maceration** in oils or alcohol.

**Natural fixatives** such as sandalwood, musk and amber were used to enhance longevity. Extracted essences were blended and aged to create complex scents. The art was well developed in ancient India and documented in classical texts such as the **Brihat Samhita**, **Gandhasara**, **Charaka Samhita** and **Sushruta Samhita**.

---

**2c. What is Pingala's binary system? (2 marks)**

Pingala's binary system appears in the **Chandahsastra**, where poetic metres are analysed using two syllable types:

- **Laghu** (short) -> 0
- **Guru** (long) -> 1

(Some texts reverse the digits; the official CIE-2 key uses Laghu = 0, Guru = 1.)

He used this system to enumerate all possible metrical patterns of n syllables (2^n patterns), effectively introducing a **binary counting system centuries before modern binary arithmetic**. Groups of three syllables gave the eight **Ganas**, which are binary words of length three.

---

**2d. What is a Tithi? (2 marks)**

A tithi is a **lunar day** in the Indian calendar, defined as the time taken for the **angular separation between the Moon and the Sun to increase by 12 degrees**. There are **30 tithis** in a lunar month, divided into:

- **Shukla Paksha** - the waxing phase (new moon to full moon), 15 tithis.
- **Krishna Paksha** - the waning phase (full moon to new moon), 15 tithis.

Because the Moon's motion is not uniform, tithis are of unequal duration.

## Question 3

**3a. Explain the features of Nagara and Dravidian temple architecture with examples. (3 marks)**

**Nagara style (North Indian)**

- Curvilinear tower called **shikhara** over the sanctum (garbhagriha).
- **Amalaka** (ribbed disc) and **kalasha** at the top.
- Square ground plan; temples often stand on raised platforms (**jagati**).
- Minimal enclosure walls; vertical emphasis; sandstone.
- Examples: **Kandariya Mahadeva Temple, Khajuraho; Sun Temple, Konark; Lingaraja Temple, Bhubaneswar**.

**Dravidian style (South Indian)**

- Pyramidal tower called **vimana**.
- Massive **gopurams** (gateway towers).
- Enclosed temple complexes with concentric walls (**prakaras**).
- Use of granite and elaborate sculpture; large temple tanks (**pushkarini**).
- Examples: **Brihadeeswara Temple, Thanjavur; Meenakshi Temple, Madurai; Ranganathaswamy Temple, Srirangam**.

---

**3b. Describe major water management practices for sustainable agriculture. (3 marks)**

**Traditional practices**

- Rainwater harvesting (tanks, ponds, farm bunds).
- Check dams and percolation tanks for groundwater recharge.
- Contour bunding and terracing to prevent runoff.
- **Khadins** and **johads** in arid regions.

**Modern practices**

- Drip irrigation (water-efficient root-zone delivery).
- Sprinkler irrigation.
- Soil moisture sensors and AI-based irrigation scheduling.
- Reuse of treated wastewater.

**Benefits**

- Reduces water wastage.
- Improves groundwater levels.
- Enhances crop productivity and climate resilience.

**3c. Explain the BMI-Consciousness Complex with a suitable example. (2 marks)**

The BMI-Consciousness complex explains the interconnected functioning of the **Body**, **Mind**, **Intellect** and **Consciousness**:

- **Body** - physical health affects mental clarity.
- **Mind** - emotions influence behaviour and physiology.
- **Intellect** - decision-making governs actions.
- **Consciousness** - awareness integrates body and mind.

Example: when a person receives a harsh remark, the senses carry it in, the **mind** feels hurt and creates a story of insult, the **intellect** evaluates the situation and chooses a measured response, and the **body** acts calmly; **consciousness** is the awareness that observes the whole reaction without being destroyed by it. With yoga, diet regulation and mindfulness, the body becomes healthier, the mind calmer and the response more balanced.

(The official key phrases the same idea through body-mass index and lifestyle: poor lifestyle raises BMI, lowering energy and increasing stress; yoga, diet and mindfulness improve BMI, calm the mind and balance consciousness.)

**3d. Describe the Triguna System and its influence on human behaviour. (2 marks)**

The Triguna system describes three fundamental qualities of nature (prakriti) that influence human behaviour:

- **Sattva (purity and harmony)** - knowledge, calmness, self-discipline; leads to wisdom and ethical conduct.
- **Rajas (activity and passion)** - desire, ambition, restlessness; drives action but causes stress if excessive.
- **Tamas (inertia and ignorance)** - laziness, confusion, dullness; leads to apathy and lack of motivation.

Influence on behaviour: human personality is a **dynamic balance of all three gunas**; spiritual practice increases Sattva; excess Rajas or Tamas produces imbalance.

**4b. Describe the Triguna system and its psychological significance. (2 marks)**

The Triguna system explains human psychology through three inherent qualities of nature:

- **Sattva** - clarity, balance, wisdom; psychological traits: calmness, empathy, self-control.
- **Rajas** - activity, desire, ambition; psychological traits: restlessness, competitiveness, stress.

The BMI-C model describes holistic human functioning through four components:

1. **Body** - physical structure and health.
2. **Mind** - emotions, feelings, sensory processing.

4. **Consciousness** - awareness and integration of body and mind.

**4d. Discuss the educational significance of the 64 Kalas. (3 marks)**

The 64 Kalas represent the comprehensive **skill-based education system** of ancient India. Significance:

- Covered arts, sciences, crafts, communication and life skills - a multidisciplinary curriculum.
- Promoted creativity, vocational competence and moral values.
- **Integrated theory with practice** (knowing and doing were not separated).
- Encouraged multidisciplinary learning rather than narrow specialisation.
- Supported personality development and self-reliance.
- Modern relevance: holistic education can integrate creativity, technical skill, communication and ethics; occupational kalas support sustainable livelihoods and preserve cultural heritage.

**Answer: (c) 1300-1600 CE**, with Madhava of Sangamagrama as its pioneer.

**10. Aryabhata's sine table used intervals of:**
(a) 1 degree (b) 3.75 degrees (c) 7.5 degrees (d) 15 degrees
**Answer: (b) 3.75 degrees**, giving 24 divisions of the quadrant.

**11. Aryabhata computed his sine table using a radius of:**
(a) 1000 arc-minutes (b) 2160 arc-minutes (c) 3438 arc-minutes (d) 3600 arc-minutes
**Answer: (c) 3438 arc-minutes**, from 21,600/(2 x pi).

**12. The English word "sine" comes from the Sanskrit jya through:**
(a) Greek "chorde" (b) Arabic "jiba/jaib" and Latin "sinus" (c) Persian "itr" (d) Chinese "chong"
**Answer: (b).** jya -> jiba -> misread jaib -> Latin sinus.

**13. Finite-difference corrections to sine computations were introduced by:**
(a) Aryabhata (b) Varahamihira (c) Nilakantha Somayaji (d) Lalla
**Answer: (c) Nilakantha Somayaji** (1444-1544 CE).

**14. The infinite series pi/4 = 1 - 1/3 + 1/5 - ... was discovered by:**
(a) Madhava (b) Brahmagupta (c) Bhaskara I (d) Baudhayana
**Answer: (a) Madhava** of Sangamagrama.

**15. The kuttaka (pulveriser) method solves:**
(a) Quadratic equations (b) Indeterminate linear equations ax + c = by (c) Cubic equations (d) Exponential equations
**Answer: (b).** It is a step-by-step algorithm close to the Euclidean algorithm.

**16. The chakravāla method of Bhaskara II solves:**
(a) Linear equations (b) Pell-type equations x^2 - Ny^2 = 1 (c) Cubic roots (d) Trigonometric identities
**Answer: (b).** For x^2 - 61y^2 = 1 it gives x = 1,766,319,049.

**17. Lilavati and Bijaganita were written by:**
(a) Aryabhata (b) Bhaskara II (c) Brahmagupta (d) Mahaviracharya
**Answer: (b) Bhaskara II.**

**18. The Brahmasphuta-siddhanta, which treats zero as a number, dates from:**
(a) 499 CE (b) 628 CE (c) 800 CE (d) 1150 CE
**Answer: (b) 628 CE**, by Brahmagupta.

**19. For a line of n syllables, the number of possible Laghu-Guru patterns is:**
(a) n^2 (b) 2n (c) 2^n (d) n!
**Answer: (c) 2^n.**

**20. Meru prastara is Pingala's version of:**
(a) The Fibonacci sequence (b) Pascal's triangle (c) The Euclidean algorithm (d) The sine table
**Answer: (b) Pascal's triangle**, i.e. binomial coefficients.

**21. Matrameru is Pingala's recursive count equivalent to:**
(a) Pascal's triangle (b) The Fibonacci sequence (c) Prime numbers (d) Geometric series
**Answer: (b) The Fibonacci sequence.**

**22. Prastara in the Chandahsastra means:**
(a) Decoding a number into a pattern (b) Enumeration of all metrical patterns (c) Finding the serial number of a pattern (d) The arithmetical triangle
**Answer: (b).** Nashta = number to pattern; uddista = pattern to number.

**23. The eight Ganas are binary words of length:**
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 8
**Answer: (b) 3.** Eight combinations: ya, ma, ta, ra, ja, bha, na, sa.

**24. Pingala's cyclic arrangement of the Ganas corresponds in modern terms to:**
(a) A Fibonacci sequence (b) A de Bruijn sequence (c) A prime sieve (d) A Pascal triangle
**Answer: (b) A de Bruijn sequence** - every 3-bit word appears exactly once in the cycle.

**25. The sum of the first n natural numbers is:**
(a) n(n+1)/2 (b) n(n-1)/2 (c) n^2 (d) n(n+1)(2n+1)/6
**Answer: (a) n(n+1)/2.** Option (d) is the sum of squares.

**26. The 10th term of 3, 7, 11, 15, ... is:**
(a) 35 (b) 39 (c) 43 (d) 40
**Answer: (b) 39**, from a + 9d = 3 + 36.

## Astronomy

**27. The Saura (solar) year is about:**
(a) 354.367 days (b) 360 days (c) 365.2564 days (d) 366 days
**Answer: (c) 365.2564 days.**

**28. The Chandra (lunar) year is about:**
(a) 354.367 days (b) 365.2564 days (c) 360 days (d) 366 days
**Answer: (a) 354.367 days**, about 11 days shorter than the solar year.

**29. Adhika masa is inserted in order to:**
(a) Mark the new year (b) Synchronise the lunar and solar years (c) Fix the weekday (d) Start the monsoon
**Answer: (b).** It is added about every 32-33 months.

**30. A tithi corresponds to an increase of how much angular separation between the Sun and Moon?**
(a) 6 degrees (b) 12 degrees (c) 13 degrees 20 minutes (d) 18 degrees
**Answer: (b) 12 degrees.** Half of a tithi (6 degrees) is a karana.

**31. The number of karanas in a lunar month is:**
(a) 11 (b) 15 (c) 27 (d) 30
**Answer: (a) 11** - four fixed and seven repeating.

**32. The ecliptic is divided into 27 nakshatras, each of:**
(a) 12 degrees (b) 13 degrees 20 minutes (c) 15 degrees (d) 30 degrees
**Answer: (b) 13 degrees 20 minutes** (360/27).

**33. A Mahayuga consists of how many years?**
(a) 432,000 (b) 1,728,000 (c) 4,320,000 (d) 4,320,000,000
**Answer: (c) 4,320,000**, made of Krita, Treta, Dvapara and Kali in the ratio 4:3:2:1.

**34. The Aryabhatiya is divided into four sections. The Golapada deals with:**
(a) Time cycles (b) Mathematics (c) Calendar calculation (d) Spherical astronomy and eclipses
**Answer: (d) Spherical astronomy**, including planetary motion and eclipses.

**35. Manda-samskara is applied to correct:**
(a) The planet's uneven speed in its orbit (b) The observer's latitude (c) The size of the Earth (d) The length of the year
**Answer: (a).** Shighra-samskara converts solar-based positions to Earth-based positions.

**36. The Panchasiddhantika was compiled by:**
(a) Aryabhata (b) Varahamihira (c) Brahmagupta (d) Lalla
**Answer: (b) Varahamihira.**

**37. The Jantar Mantar observatories were built by:**
(a) Bhaskara II (b) Sawai Jai Singh II (c) Raja Raja Chola (d) Krishnadevaraya
**Answer: (b) Sawai Jai Singh II**, at Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Varanasi and Mathura.

**38. The Gola Yantra is:**
(a) A water clock (b) An armillary sphere (c) A gnomon (d) A sundial
**Answer: (b) An armillary sphere**, representing the celestial sphere with rings.

**39. The Shanku (gnomon) is used to measure:**
(a) Time by water flow (b) Shadow length and direction (c) Angular height by a disc (d) Weight of metals
**Answer: (b).** From the shadow it gives local noon, the Sun's altitude and latitude.

## Engineering and technology

**40. Zawar in Rajasthan is famous for the ancient production of:**
(a) Gold (b) Zinc (c) Silver (d) Tin
**Answer: (b) Zinc**, by downward distillation in the 9th-13th centuries CE.

**41. Brass is an alloy of:**
(a) Copper and tin (b) Copper and zinc (c) Iron and carbon (d) Copper and gold
**Answer: (b) Copper and zinc.** Bronze is copper and tin.

**42. In ancient India, brass was prepared using copper and:**
(a) Calamine, a zinc ore (b) Galena (c) Cinnabar (d) Hematite
**Answer: (a) Calamine.** Galena is a lead ore, cinnabar a mercury ore.

**43. Silver was obtained from galena by:**
(a) Panning (b) Cupellation (c) Amalgamation (d) Electrolysis
**Answer: (b) Cupellation** - oxidising away the lead to leave silver.

**44. Mercury was extracted from:**
(a) Cinnabar (HgS) (b) Galena (PbS) (c) Bauxite (d) Magnetite
**Answer: (a) Cinnabar**, by roasting and distillation (Dhelki and Patana yantras).

**45. The Delhi Iron Pillar is remarkable because:**
(a) It is made of cast iron (b) It has remained corrosion-free for over 1600 years (c) It is 20 m tall (d) It is made of bronze
**Answer: (b).** It is 7.2 m, over 6 tonnes, 99.7 % wrought iron, protected by a phosphate layer and low phosphorus.

**46. Kanta-loha, Tikshna-loha and Musta-loha refer respectively to:**
(a) Steel, soft iron, cast iron (b) Soft iron, steel, cast iron (c) Cast iron, steel, soft iron (d) Bronze, brass, steel
**Answer: (b) Soft iron, steel, cast iron.**

**47. The crucible carburisation process took about 6 hours, compared with the European cementation process which took:**
(a) 6-20 minutes (b) 6-20 hours (c) 6-20 days (d) 6-20 weeks
**Answer: (c) 6-20 days**, which is why the Indian process was far more efficient.

**48. Lost-wax casting is known in India as:**
(a) Madhucchishta Vidhana (b) Rasavidya (c) Vastu Vidya (d) Gandhayukti
**Answer: (a) Madhucchishta Vidhana.**

**49. Panchaloha, the sacred five-metal alloy, contains:**
(a) Gold, silver, copper, tin, zinc (b) Iron, copper, tin, lead, zinc (c) Gold, copper, iron, lead, tin (d) Silver, gold, brass, bronze, steel
**Answer: (a) Gold, silver, copper, tin and zinc.**

**50. The "Dancing Girl" figurine from Mohenjo-daro is an example of:**
(a) Stone sculpture (b) Lost-wax bronze casting (c) Terracotta painting (d) Wootz forging
**Answer: (b) Lost-wax bronze casting** (c. 2500 BCE).

**51. Indigo is obtained from:**
(a) Rubia cordifolia (b) Indigofera tinctoria (c) Curcuma longa (d) Crocus sativus
**Answer: (b) Indigofera tinctoria.** Madder gives red, turmeric yellow, saffron orange.

**52. Alum, iron salts and tannins used in dyeing are examples of:**
(a) Pigments (b) Binders (c) Mordants (d) Fixatives
**Answer: (c) Mordants** - they fix the dye to the fibre and modify the shade.

**53. Bandhani and Ikat are examples of:**
(a) Direct dyeing (b) Resist dyeing (c) Mordant dyeing (d) Fresco painting
**Answer: (b) Resist dyeing** - areas are tied, waxed or covered before dyeing.

**54. The Ajanta paintings are examples of:**
(a) Miniature painting (b) Wall painting / mural (c) Folk art (d) Fresco on wet plaster only
**Answer: (b) Wall painting / mural** on lime-plastered surfaces. (Ajanta is often described as tempera-like mural work rather than true wet fresco.)

**55. The deg-bhapka method is associated with:**
(a) Zinc distillation (b) Perfume distillation (c) Indigo dyeing (d) Lost-wax casting
**Answer: (b) Perfume distillation** - copper pot (deg) and receiver (bhapka), still used at Kannauj.

**56. The word "attar" derives from the Sanskrit:**
(a) Gandha (b) Sugandha (c) Dhupa (d) Taila
**Answer: (a) Gandha**, through Persian "itr".

**57. India's "perfume capital", where deg-bhapka distillation survives, is:**
(a) Kannauj (b) Kanchipuram (c) Khajuraho (d) Kollam
**Answer: (a) Kannauj** in Uttar Pradesh.
