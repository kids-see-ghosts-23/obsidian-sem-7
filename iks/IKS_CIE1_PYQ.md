## Question 1

**1a. Describe the six schools of Indian philosophy (3 marks).**
- Nyaya - School of logic and epistemology (founded by Gautama).
- Vaisheshika - School of atomism and naturalism (founded by Kanada).
- Samkhya - School of enumeration and dualism of matter (Prakriti) and spirit (Purusha) (founded by Kapila).
- Yoga - School of practice and meditation (systematized by Patanjali).
- Purva Mimamsa - School of Vedic ritual interpretation (founded by Jaimini).
- Vedanta (Uttara Mimamsa) - School of spiritual knowledge and realization of Brahman (founded by Badarayana).

**1b. Mention any two threats to traditional knowledge (2+2 marks).**
- Biopiracy and loss of oral traditions are among the most significant threats endangering the preservation and continuation of traditional knowledge systems worldwide. Biopiracy is the commercial exploitation of naturally occurring biochemicals or genetic materials (e.g., MNC patenting of turmeric's medicinal qualities used in Ayurveda for thousands of years).
- Language threat: indigenous languages (e.g., Sanskrit, which preserves Ayurveda) suffer colonial exploitation and modern lifestyles; External pressures: migration, globalisation, disruption of traditional ways of life.

**1c. Differentiate between the traditional Indian system of knowledge and the Western system of knowledge (3 marks).**
- Traditional Indian System of Knowledge: emphasizes holistic, spiritual, and experiential understanding - knowledge is seen as a means to realize truth (Satya) and harmony between self, nature, and the universe.
- Western System of Knowledge: focuses on analytical, empirical, and objective understanding - knowledge is pursued through observation, experimentation, and logical reasoning to control or explain nature.

**1d. Explain how the Vedic Corpus forms the foundation of Indian knowledge traditions (3 marks).**
The Vedic Corpus is the earliest and most authoritative source of wisdom in India. It integrates spiritual, philosophical, and scientific ideas, introducing concepts like Rita (cosmic order) and Dharma (moral law). The Vedas laid the basis for later systems of philosophy, medicine, astronomy, grammar, and ethics, providing both the content and methods of inquiry that shaped the entire Indian intellectual tradition.

**2c. Explain the two types of perception (Pratyaksha) in Nyaya philosophy (3 marks).**
The Nyaya philosophy recognizes two types of perception:
1. Laukika (Ordinary) Perception - perception through the five senses (sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch) in everyday experience.
2. Alaukika (Extraordinary) Perception - non-ordinary perception, which includes: Samanyalakshana pratyaksha (perceiving universals), Jnanalakshana pratyaksha (knowledge-based perception), and Yogaja pratyaksha (perception through yogic power).

**2d. Differentiate agricultural and ecological traditional knowledge (2 marks).**
Agricultural traditional knowledge focuses on sustaining farming systems, while ecological traditional knowledge emphasizes maintaining environmental balance and biodiversity.

## Question 5

**5a. Explain the standardized street widths of the Indus-Saraswati civilization (2 marks).**
Indus-Saraswati civilization street widths were standardized according to a well-planned urban grid system. At sites like Mohenjo-Daro and Harappa: Main streets: about 9 to 10 meters (30-33 feet) wide; Secondary streets: about 4 to 5 meters (13-16 feet) wide; Lanes or by-lanes: about 1.8 to 2.4 meters (6-8 feet) wide. These uniform measurements indicate the use of a standardized unit of length, likely based on the Indus cubit (about 51.8 cm), reflecting advanced urban planning and civil engineering.

# SECTION C - UNIT-1 SUBJECTIVE QUESTION BANK - SOLVED

**1. Explain the meaning of Traditional knowledge (2 marks).**
Traditional knowledge or indigenous knowledge is developed by local indigenous communities that are well-developed and whose long-standing customs and practices are followed by these communities from generation to generation. Studies into the origins, behaviour, and development of humanism in both prehistoric and modern societies led to the recognition of traditional knowledge. The adoption of traditional knowledge by international organisations, like the UN, has resulted in its widespread appreciation and recognition.

**2. Explain any three threats to traditional knowledge (3 marks).**
- Language threat: Indigenous languages and dialects preserve enormous amounts of traditional knowledge, such as Ayurveda in Sanskrit. These languages have been at the receiving end of colonial exploitation and modern lifestyles.
- External pressures: The traditional methods of preserving or transmitting knowledge to future generations are threatened by migration, environmental pressures, the impact of modern lifestyles, disruption of traditional ways of life, and globalisation.
- Biopiracy: The commercial exploitation of biochemicals or genetic materials that occur naturally.

**3. Explain the need to protect traditional knowledge (3 marks).**
- Protecting Indigenous culture and identities: Traditional knowledge and languages are a significant way to maintain and preserve indigenous cultures and identities and promote well-being.
- Protecting livelihoods: Traditional knowledge is one of the sources of livelihood for indigenous people, which must be protected.
- Health benefits: As traditional knowledge is connected with environment and spirituality, it is important to well-being; traditional medicines can provide health benefits to a large population, like in India.
(Ecological benefits: TK presents enormous opportunities to conserve forests and biodiversity - e.g., sacred groves.)

**4. List the different types of traditional knowledge (3 marks).**
Agricultural, ecological, medicinal (Ayurveda, Siddha), technical/craft (metallurgy, textiles, architecture), and environmental conservation knowledge (sacred groves).

**5. Explain the transition from traditional to Western knowledge (3 marks).**
The transition from traditional to Western knowledge in India marked a profound shift in the way learning was conceived, transmitted, and institutionalized. Traditional Indian education, rooted in the Gurukula system and temple-based learning centers such as Nalanda and Takshashila, emphasized holistic development - blending philosophy, science, arts, and ethics within the framework of Sanatana Dharma. Knowledge was viewed as sacred (Vidya), aimed at self-realization and societal harmony rather than material gain. Oral traditions, Sanskrit texts, and regional commentaries ensured the continuity of indigenous sciences, including Ayurveda, astronomy, mathematics, metallurgy, and linguistics. With British colonial rule, education underwent Westernization through the English language, new curricula, and modern institutions; the focus shifted to empirical, utilitarian, job-oriented learning, creating a divide - but also enabling documentation and global exposure of Indian heritage, paving the way for a contemporary synthesis.

**6. List any three Indian mathematicians and their works (3 marks).**
- Aryabhata (476-550 CE): Authored the Aryabhatiya. Introduced zero as a number in a positional number system; accurately approximated pi to about 3.1416; significant contributions to trigonometry and algebra.
- Brahmagupta (598-668 CE): Authored the Brahmasphutasiddhanta. Developed rules for arithmetic operations involving zero and negative numbers; methods for solving quadratic equations; contributions to astronomy and geometry.
- Bhaskara II (1114-1185 CE): Authored the Siddhanta Shiromani. Known for work in algebra, calculus, and number systems; described concepts related to differential calculus centuries before Isaac Newton.

**7. List any two ancient educational institutions (2 marks).**
- Nalanda: Located in present-day Bihar; operated from the 5th to the 12th century AD; renowned cultural and intellectual hub attracting students from across Asia.
- Takshashila: Located in ancient Gandhara (modern-day Pakistan); a major center for higher learning, sometimes cited as the world's oldest university, active as early as the 6th century BC.

**8. List any two ancient architectural texts (2 marks).**
Manasara, Brihat Samhita, Mayamata, Anka Shastra, Aparajita Vastu Shastra (any two).

**9. List the six classical schools of Indian philosophy.**
Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa, and Vedanta.

**10. Draw a neat framework for the classification of the IKS Corpus (3 marks).**
IKS Corpus = Literary Part (Sanatana Dharma Corpus [Vedas, Vedangas + supplementary literature] and Other Dharmik Literature [Buddhist, Jain]) + Non-Literary Part (oral traditions, health traditions, performing arts, 64 Kalas).

**11. List any two contributions of Buddhist and Jain literature (2 marks).**
- Buddhist literature incorporated mathematical concepts and extended into practical sciences such as maritime activity, shipbuilding, and alchemy. Notable example: Rasaratnakara by Nagarjuna (1st century CE), one of the earliest treatises on alchemy.
- Jain literature includes canonical sacred texts integrating mathematics as a core discipline (Ganitanuyoga). Works such as the Tattvartha Sutra and later explorations by Madhavacharya attempt to quantify complex natural phenomena - e.g., estimating the number of species as 2^96.

**12. What are the Vedas (2 marks)?**
Veda means "Knowledge." It is a Sanskrit word from the root "Vid," which means finding, knowing, acquiring, or understanding. The term Veda is the treasure house of all knowledge and all Shastras.

**13. List the four Vedas.**
Rig Veda, Yajur Veda, Sama Veda, and Atharva Veda.

**14. List the four major text types/portions of the Vedas (2 marks).**
- The Samhitas: the most ancient layer, consisting of mantras, hymns, prayers, and benedictions, which in literary terms put together or joined the other three texts.
- The Aranyakas: constitute the philosophy behind the ritual sacrifice.
- The Brahmanas: commentary on the hymns of the four Vedas.
- The Upanishads: conversations between teachers and students that clarify the Vedas' philosophical message.

**15. What are Samhitas and Aranyakas (2 marks)?**
The Samhitas are the most ancient layer of text in the Vedas, consisting of mantras, hymns, prayers, and benedictions; the Aranyakas constitute the philosophy behind the ritual sacrifice.

**16. What are Brahmanas and Upanishads?**
The Brahmanas contain commentary on the hymns of the four Vedas; the Upanishads consist of conversations between teachers and students that clarify the Vedas' philosophical message.

**17. Explain any three differences between Vedas and Vedangas (3 marks).**
1. Purpose: The Vedas are holy texts that teach about the origin of the world and the reason for existence. The Vedangas are ancillary studies that help maintain the integrity of the Vedic tradition.
2. Structure: The Vedas are divided into four collections - Rig, Sama, Yajur, and Atharva. The Vedangas are six: Shiksha, Chandas, Vyakarana, Nirukta, Jyotisha, and Kalpa.
3. Content: The Vedas contain hymns, incantations, and rituals. The Vedangas include insights into meters, grammar, and linguistic analysis.
(4. Origin: Vedas circa 1500-1200 BCE; Vedangas are the last treatises of Vedic literature.)

**18. List any two Kalpasutras.**
(i) Shrauta-sutras, dealing with Shrauta sacrifices. (ii) Grihya-sutras, dealing with domestic ceremonies. (Also: Dharma-sutras - religious and social laws; Shulba-sutras - rules of measurement of fire-altars.)

**19. List any two nastika schools of Indian philosophy.**
Charvaka, Bauddha, and Jaina (any two).

**20. List the three major sub-schools of Vedanta (2 marks).**
1. Advaita (non-dualism) - holds that the individual soul (Atman) and Brahman are ultimately one.
2. Visishtadvaita - teaches that the Supreme Being (Vishnu) has definite attributes; the world and individual souls are distinct yet inseparable modes of Brahman.
3. Dvaita - posits three separate realities: Vishnu, the eternal soul, and matter.

**17. Differentiate between the Bhutasamkhya system and the Katapayadi system (any three differences).**
1. Bhutasamkhya encodes each digit as a whole word (name of an entity/being); Katapayadi encodes each digit as a consonant letter within a word.
2. Bhutasamkhya needs many synonyms per digit and becomes verbose for large numbers; Katapayadi is compact - one letter per digit.
3. Katapayadi allows flexible, meaningful word formation (vowels carry no value; conjunct consonants take the last one), ideal for oral memorization; Bhutasamkhya relies on fixed entity-number associations.

- Very efficient method of presenting results of complex calculations using number symbols and then codifying and committing to memory effortlessly.
